01 · The piano

It all starts at the piano.

Piano came first, and years of it before I recorded anything. Then I started teaching it — which is where the ear actually got trained, because you can't fix a sound you can't explain to someone else. I play flute as well. Every instrument I've learned is one more voice I can pick out inside a mix.

The lessons paid for the rest — my first album was written, played and produced in my room at my parents' house.

[How long you've taught, and the record's name and year.]

02 · The Hague

The Royal Conservatoire, The Hague.

I moved abroad to study sound properly, and I'm finishing that degree there now. Formal training gave structure to an ear that had already been working for years: acoustics, signal flow, critical listening, and studio practice alongside people who do this for a living.

[Specialisation, graduation year, and any credits or projects from the programme.]

03 · The choir

I conduct a choir. And I still teach.

Standing in front of a room of singers and making them arrive together is the same job as a mix: balance, blend, and knowing who to bring forward. I teach guitar now as well as piano — it keeps my ear honest, because you can't explain a sound you don't understand.

[Which choir, how many voices, how long.]

04 · Now

All of it ends up in the mix.

Producer, teacher, conductor, engineer. It's why a record I finish sounds like the room it was made in — and like the people who made it.
