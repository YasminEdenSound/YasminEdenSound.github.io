01 · The bedroom

I made the first record in my bedroom.

I wrote, played and produced my first album in my room at my parents' house. No studio, no booth, no budget — one room and whatever I could get a microphone in front of.

[The record's name, the year, and what you recorded it on.]

02 · Paying for it

Piano lessons paid for it.

I taught piano to fund the work. Every hour at someone else's keyboard bought another hour at my own — and it's where the patience for other people's records came from.

03 · Leaving

Then I left everyone I knew.

It wasn't going to happen where I was. So I went — abroad, on my own, to study music properly.

[One line on the decision: where you left, who you left, what it cost.]

04 · The Hague

The Royal Conservatoire, The Hague.

I'm finishing my degree in sound there now — the formal half of an ear that had already been working for years.

05 · The choir

I conduct a choir.

Standing in front of a room of singers and making them arrive together is the same job as a mix: balance, blend, and knowing who to bring forward.

[Which choir, how many voices, how long.]

06 · Six strings

And I still teach.

Guitar now, as well as piano. Teaching keeps my ear honest — you can't explain a sound you don't actually understand.

07 · Now

All of it ends up in the mix.

Bedroom producer, teacher, conductor, engineer. It's why a record I finish sounds like the room it was made in — and like the people who made it.
